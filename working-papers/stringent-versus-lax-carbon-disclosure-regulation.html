Working Paper

Stringent versus Lax Carbon Disclosure Regulation: Implications for Greenwashing and Welfare

with Yulan Wang, Christopher S. Tang, and Xiaoshuai Fan.

Back to Working Papers

Abstract

Carbon disclosure regulations vary across jurisdictions: “stringent” regulations mandate the explicit disclosure of internal (Scope 1 and 2) emission, whereas “lax” regulations grant firms discretion over their reporting formats. This divergence gives rise to three disclosure formats in practice: partial disclosure (internal emission only), granular disclosure (internal and supplier emission reported separately), and aggregated disclosure (internal and supplier emission combined). Under stringent mandates, firms are restricted to partial or granular disclosure; under lax regimes, they choose between partial and aggregated disclosure. To examine how disclosure regulations shape a firm's optimal disclosure and pricing decisions, we develop a parsimonious model in which environmentally conscious consumers evaluate carbon disclosures using mental accounting. We show that, regardless of regulatory stringency, a firm with low internal emission can strategically conceal high supplier emission through “greenwashing” whenever possible. When partial disclosure is chosen, stringent regulations induce the firm to charge a high price and serve only naive consumers. By contrast, under lax regulations, sufficiently low internal emission can induce the firm to charge a low price and serve both naive and rational consumers. Furthermore, whether lax or stringent regulation can achieve a lower greenwashing probability and higher social welfare depends on the prevalence of naive consumers. Contrary to conventional wisdom, stringent disclosure rules do not guarantee superior outcomes. Our analysis identifies key conditions under which lax regulation is surprisingly more effective in curbing greenwashing and enhancing welfare. Incorporating consumer loss aversion can expand the region where lax regulation outperforms stringent mandates on both fronts.
