Working Paper

Returnless Refunds: Combating Fraudulent Returns in Competitive Markets

with Yulan Wang and Tingliang Huang.

Back to Working Papers

Abstract

Many platforms now offer returnless refunds, allowing consumers to receive refunds for defective products without returning them. This may encourage fraudulent returns, whereby consumers falsely claim that well-functioning products are defective. This study examines how the adoption of fraud detection technology affects the implications of returnless refunds in competitive markets. A game-theoretic model is developed in which a platform mandates returnless refunds for two competing sellers with heterogeneous product defect rates. The results show that the existence of fraudulent returns may force the low-defect-rate seller to exit the market, leading to a lemon-market outcome. Detection accuracy has a non-monotonic effect on fraudulent returns: fraud can be eliminated even under low detection accuracy when consumers face moderate return hassle costs. Although higher detection accuracy benefits the high-defect-rate seller, it may reduce the profits of the low-defect-rate seller and the platform, as well as consumer surplus and social welfare. Extensions show that mandatory returnless refunds can outperform voluntary seller opt-in in platform profit and social welfare under certain conditions, whereas voluntary opt-in can prevent the lemon-market outcome. These findings provide managerial insights for the returnless refund policy design, seller participation, and detection technology deployment.
